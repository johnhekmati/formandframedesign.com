    Straight talk

    What public reviews keep saying — answered neutrally.

    Themes pulled from public Google / Yelp-style feedback across locations. No spin — just how to get the best of a busy hometown chain.

    Why does the drive-thru feel slow at peak?

    Volume is real — morning taco runs and lunch rushes stack cars. If you’re on a clock, order ahead online, try curbside, or hit a later window. Peak pain shows up most often in low-star drive-thru notes; off-peak visits get praised for speed.

    My order was wrong — what helps?

    Order accuracy is the other recurring low-star theme. Check the bag before you pull away, especially on mixed taco orders. If something’s off, call that location’s number from the finder above — they can usually correct it faster in person than through a delivery app.

    Why do people complain about salsa?

    Regulars love the salsa; low-star notes often mention small cups or the wrong color (red vs green). Ask clearly at the window — “extra red” or “extra green” — and confirm before you leave. It’s a high-frequency ask at a high-volume counter.

    Is every location the same?

    Public reviews vary by door — crews, rush timing, and ticket accuracy aren’t identical across sixteen huts. If one location misses, try another nearby; 5-star regulars often name a “home” store they stick with.

    What do the 5-star regulars keep shouting out?

    All-day breakfast tacos (bean & cheese, bacon & egg, papa ranchero, chorizo & papa), fair prices, packed portions, green enchiladas, barbacoa, and friendly crews when the line is moving. Value-for-flavor is the through-line.

    Do you take online orders?

    Yes — start at order.online for Tia's, then pick your location. Delivery apps also list many doors; for accuracy and salsa extras, ordering direct or through the drive-thru still wins for a lot of locals.
